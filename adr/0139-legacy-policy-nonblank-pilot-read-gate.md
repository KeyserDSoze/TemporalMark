The v1 offline ZIP inspector at `verify_period_export.py` explicitly requires `PolicyOid` be a nonblank string (Python `bool(value.strip())`), even when all raw TSR/SHA256 and canonical document imprint metadata are locally consistent. App-owned new issuance/writer admission already refuses empty/whitespace provider `PolicyOid` (`IsRequiredOriginalTextAdmissible`). **V001 has only `policy_oid text NOT NULL`**, and `TimestampRecord.Validate` intentionally checks provider identity, imprint algorithm, DER hash/UTC/IDs but **not `PolicyOid`**. The #180 authorized pilot read gates already check original DER/hash, status `Unverified`, canonical document SHA-256/OID, tenant+requested ID+period, metadata/DER limits, order and duplicates, but **do not** check nonblank policy metadata. Thus an old, restored or privileged owner-inserted `Unverified` record with original self-consistent DER/SHA and an empty/whitespace policy may be served via owner Page/Receipt/Search or included in a ZIP the *same project's offline inspector rejects*. This is a hypothetical legacy read-consistency risk, not evidence of an invalid real signed TSA, malice or legal qualification.

## What the policy gate does NOT prove

This checks **nonblank original text only**, not an OID's lexical grammar, ASN.1 syntax, signed RFC3161 `TSTInfo.policy` match, `messageImprint` CMS signature binding, signer chain, revocation, historical EU Trusted List qualified service status, WORM/legal archiving, indefinite GDPR lawful retention or complete-period coverage. A privileged DB owner can bypass application writer constraints; authorized read denial is a detection/review interlock, **not** a repair to historical evidence. Future approved alternative policy algorithms/validations require independent governance rather than silently broadening the limited pilot.
